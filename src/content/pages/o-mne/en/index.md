I'm a physicist, mother of two, outdoor enthusiast, and a musician from the Janáček Conservatory in Ostrava — yes, in roughly that order, going by how much time each one takes up these days.

Before the kids, we went canyoning and mountain hiking whenever the weather allowed. We're trying the same things with them now, just slower, and with a bigger backpack full of spare clothes.

I spent four years at the conservatory before physics took over. I didn't stay with music, but the patience and the feel for rhythm and sentence structure it taught me still serve me now that I'm assembling sentences instead of notes.

At CERN, alongside the research, I also volunteered as a guide, leading visitors through the accelerator — and found that explaining high-energy physics to a curious visitor is harder, and more interesting, than writing a paper about it. It convinced me that complicated things can be explained clearly without frightening the reader or the listener. It's a skill I use in writing now as much as I once did in science.

The professional version of me — what exactly I did at CERN and the European Spallation Source — lives on the [Publications](/en/publikace/) page.
